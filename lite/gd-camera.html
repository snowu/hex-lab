Cameras, screen shake and parallax

A 2D camera that follows well, shakes with “trauma”, and does not make pixel art shimmer.

Follow cameras

  • Locked to the player: simple, but every micro-movement jitters the whole screen.
  • Dead zone: the camera only moves when the player leaves a box in the middle.
  • Look-ahead: shift the target in the direction of motion so the player sees more of where they are going.
  • Smoothing: use the frame-rate independent exponential from the interpolation lesson, not a fixed lerp factor.
  • Room cameras (Celeste, Zelda): snap or slide between fixed rooms; the design controls what the player sees.
  • Limits: clamp to the map bounds so the camera never shows outside the level.

Screen shake with trauma

A good model (from Squirrel Eiserloh's GDC talk on juicing cameras): keep a trauma value from 0 to 1. Events add to it, it decays linearly over time, and the shake amount is trauma² or trauma³, so small hits barely register and big ones hit hard. Drive offset (and a little rotation) with smooth noise rather than pure random to avoid a harsh buzz. Add a setting to scale or disable it.

Parallax

Layers further away move slower than the camera. Move each by camera_x * (1 - depth) (depth 0 is foreground, 1 is infinitely far, stationary). Keep layers on whole pixels for pixel art.

Pixel art cameras

If the game renders at a small base resolution and is scaled up (see the pixel art lessons), keep the camera on integer coordinates or sprites will “swim” as they cross sub-pixel boundaries. Smooth in float, then round for rendering. Godot: enable pixel snapping for 2D transforms and the nearest texture filter.
