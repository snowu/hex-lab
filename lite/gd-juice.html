Game feel and juice

Why two games with identical rules can feel completely different, and the small tools that make an action satisfying.

Game feel

Steve Swink (Game Feel) splits it into: real-time control (input is immediate and responsive), simulated space (the world has weight and collision that makes sense) and polish (effects that sell the action). The first two are design and engineering; the third is “juice”.

Juice

Juice is abundant, redundant feedback to an action: the player did something, so the whole game reacts. The two talks to watch are Juice it or lose it (Jonasson and Purho) and The art of screenshake (Nijman, Vlambeer). Common tools:

  • Screen shake, short and decaying, scaled with the impact. Always give players a setting to reduce or disable it.
  • Hit stop (a few frames of freeze on contact): the single cheapest way to make a hit feel heavy.
  • Squash and stretch on landing and jumping, and a quick scale pulse on pickups.
  • Particles: dust on landing, sparks on hits.
  • Flash the sprite white for a frame on damage.
  • Sound: a punchy layered sound with a little random pitch variation so repeated sounds do not fatigue. Audio is half of the feel.
  • Anticipation and follow-through in animation: wind up, strike, recover.
  • Camera: slight lead in the move direction, zoom punch on big events.

Rules of thumb

  • Juice multiplies a good loop. It does not rescue a bad one.
  • Scale feedback to importance: a coin pickup should not shake the screen like a boss death.
  • Keep the effects readable: do not hide gameplay information under particles.
  • Respect accessibility: flashing, shake and motion options.
  • Do the sim and effects in separate layers so juice never changes gameplay results (hit stop that pauses the game logic is fine; effects that move hitboxes are not).

Simulation: The same click, with and without juice

Click or tap the green block. Underneath, the game only adds 1 to a counter. Turn the feedback layers on one by one.

Try this in the full app:

  • With everything off, click a few times. Technically correct, emotionally dead.
  • Turn on hitstop only: the click feels heavier even though nothing visible was added.
  • Add squash and flash, then particles.
  • Turn on sound (a synthesized blip). Notice how much of the impression comes from audio.
  • Press all on and reflect: would this be too much for a coin pickup?

Answer key

1. Quick check back ↑

B. Hit stop (freezing a few frames on contact) combined with a short sound and flash

A few frames of freeze communicates mass and impact. It is a standard in fighting and action games.

2. Quick check back ↑

B. Scale feedback to the importance of the event and offer options to reduce shake

Juice should be proportional and optional. Reserve strong effects for big moments.

3. Check 1 back ↑

B. Real-time control, simulated space and polish: responsiveness, weight and feedback sell the same rules

Game feel comes from immediate control, a world with weight, and redundant polish feedback, not from the rules alone.

4. Check 2 back ↑

A. Juice must live in a separate layer so effects never change gameplay results

Effects sell the action but must not move hitboxes or decide outcomes; hit stop pausing logic is fine, effects altering results are not.
