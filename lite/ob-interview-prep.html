Preparing for the Neonomics interview

Research checklist, questions to ask, and how to map your experience.

Neonomics is an Oslo-based open banking company (founded 2017) with a licensed payment institution, an aggregator API covering many European banks, and a pay-by-bank product (Nello Pay). It has been growing via partnerships and acquisitions. Check their latest news; details change.

✅ Research checklist (do these before the interview)

  1. Read the API docs: Platform overview, Consent/Authorization/SCA, Error handling, Pay by Bank. Draw the flow from memory.
  2. Skim the blog and the About page for strategy: what are they pushing right now (pay by bank, data products, UK expansion)?
  3. Look up their engineering job posts for the stack and the on-call/ownership language.
  4. Read the Berlin Group NextGenPSD2 intro (free) and the EBA PSD2 RTS summary.
  5. Try a sandbox for any open banking API to get a feel for consent redirects.

🎯 Likely themes

  • Reliability across flaky third-party banks (timeouts, breakers, bulkheads).
  • Idempotent payments and exactly-once effects.
  • Security: certificates, signing, secrets, least privilege, GDPR.
  • Data modelling for normalised accounts/transactions across banks.
  • Asynchronous status handling, webhooks, reconciliation.
  • Working in a regulated environment: auditability, change management, incident reports.
  • AI: where it helps (support triage, error-explanation, categorisation, dev productivity) and where it must not touch money flows without controls.

❓ Good questions to ask them

  • “What fails most often today: banks, our infra, or the integration with customers? How do you detect bank-side regressions?”
  • “How is on-call organised and what did your last significant incident teach you?”
  • “How do you test against hundreds of banks: contract tests, sandboxes, synthetic probes?”
  • “How do you balance building canonical models against exposing bank-specific features?”
  • “How do you use AI in engineering and what are the guardrails in a regulated setting?”
  • “What does a senior engineer own here in the first 6 months?”

🧩 Map your experience

For each, prepare a story: (1) a third-party dependency that was unreliable, (2) a money or data-correctness bug, (3) a security/compliance requirement you implemented, (4) a migration or integration across many heterogeneous partners, (5) something you simplified. Tie each to a concept above.

Quick check

Interviewer: “How would you make sure our API never double-charges a customer?” Pick the strongest outline.
  1. A. Check the database before inserting
  2. B. Idempotency keys scoped per customer with a unique constraint, atomic claim+effect (or outbox), safe retries with backoff, status reconciliation against the bank, and monitoring for duplicates
