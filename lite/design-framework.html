System Design · lesson 1 of 4 · 5 min

A framework you can run under pressure

Requirements → estimates → API → data → architecture → deep dives → failure.

Interviewers do not want your memorised architecture. They want to watch you structure an open problem. Step through the framework below, then practise it on the worked design in the next lessons.

1. Clarify & scope (3-5 min)

Functional requirements (the 3-4 things the system must do) and what is explicitly out of scope. Ask who the users are and what “correct” means. Money? Then correctness beats latency.

2. Non-functional & numbers

Availability target, latency target (p50/p99), consistency needs, durability, data retention, regulatory limits. Back-of-envelope: requests/sec = users × actions/day ÷ 86,400, peak ≈ 3-10× average. Storage = items × size × retention.

10M users × 5 actions/day = 50M/day ≈ 580 rps average, ~3k rps peak

3. API & data model

Sketch 3-5 endpoints (with idempotency on writes), and the core entities with their keys. State your read/write ratio: it drives storage and caching choices.

4. High-level design

Client → edge/LB → stateless services → data stores, plus async paths (queues, workers). Keep boxes few and each one justified by a requirement.

5. Deep dives

Pick the 1-2 hardest parts (the interviewer will nudge you). Typical: data partitioning, hot keys, exactly-once effects, fan-out, ranking, consistency between stores.

6. Failure, scale, operate

What breaks first? Retries, timeouts, backpressure, degradation modes, observability (RED/USE metrics, traces), rollout/rollback, cost. This step is what separates senior from mid.
