Consent, SCA & security model

SCA flavours, eIDAS certificates, signing, consent lifetime, GDPR.

SCA flows

  • Redirect: user is sent to the bank’s site/app to authenticate (most common).
  • Decoupled: you start it, the user approves in their banking app (push), you poll or get a callback.
  • Embedded: you collect credentials: rare and discouraged, security-heavy.

Certificates & request security

  • eIDAS certificates identify a TPP to banks. QWAC (website authentication certificate) is used for mutual TLS; QSealC (seal certificate) is used to sign requests (message-level integrity and non-repudiation). Neonomics’ docs refer to QSeal signature headers.
  • Know the difference: mTLS secures the channel and identifies the party; a signature proves a specific message was not altered and came from the key holder, even through proxies.
  • Private key handling: HSM/KMS, no keys in repos or images, rotation plan, and monitoring on certificate expiry (an expired cert = every bank call fails).

Consent lifetime

Under the PSD2 RTS, unattended AIS access (user not present) is limited to 4 requests per 24 hours per account per TPP, and the SCA exemption for repeat account-information access was extended from 90 to 180 days in 2023, so users must re-authenticate periodically. Product impact: cache sensibly, refresh in batches, and design a graceful “reconnect your bank” UX. Banks may implement the details differently, so verify per bank.

Data protection

Financial data is personal data (GDPR): purpose limitation, data minimisation, retention limits, encryption at rest and in transit, access logging, deletion on consent withdrawal. Never log tokens, full IBANs or transaction descriptions in plain application logs.

What comes after PSD2: PSD3 and the PSR

The EU is replacing PSD2 with two texts: PSD3, a directive mainly about licensing and supervision, and the Payment Services Regulation (PSR), which holds most of the conduct rules and applies directly in every member state (and through the EEA agreement in Norway), so banks interpret it less differently than PSD2. Status at the time of writing (late 2026): political agreement in November 2025 and agreed final texts in April 2026, with formal adoption and publication still pending. The rules apply roughly a year and a half to two years after entry into force, with some fraud rules later. Check the current status before the interview.

Question to expect: “A user withdraws consent. What happens in your system?”

  • Revoke on the bank side if an API exists; delete the session/consent record.
  • Stop all scheduled refreshes for that consent immediately (this is a correctness issue, not cleanup).
  • Delete or anonymise stored account data per policy, keeping only what law requires (audit trail).
  • Make deletion idempotent and observable (an audit event), and propagate to caches, backups policy, analytics and downstream customers.

Answer key

1. Quick check back ↑

B. Treat bank-side revocation as a normal event: detect it from the bank’s responses, stop scheduled refreshes, mark the consent revoked, and notify the customer through a webhook

Consent state now changes outside your system. Treat the bank’s “consent revoked” or “consent invalid” response as a state transition, the same way as error 1426 starts consent, not as a transient failure to retry.

2. Quick check back ↑

B. QSealC

QSealC is for signing messages. QWAC authenticates the TLS connection (mTLS). Some banks and aggregators need both.

3. Quick check back ↑

B. Expired or revoked TPP certificate (or an intermediate CA change)

Simultaneous failure across independent banks points to something on your side shared by all of them: your certificate. That is why you alert on expiry weeks ahead and keep a renewal runbook.

4. Check 1 back ↑

D. The signature proves that exact message was unaltered by the key holder end to end, while mTLS only secures each channel hop and identifies the party

mTLS secures the channel and authenticates the party; a QSealC signature gives message-level integrity and non-repudiation through intermediaries.

5. Check 2 back ↑

B. Hourly unattended refreshes exceed the roughly 4-requests-per-24h limit, and full IBANs in plain logs breach data minimisation

Unattended AIS access is quota-limited, so cache, batch, and design a reconnect UX; financial data is GDPR personal data, so minimise logging.
