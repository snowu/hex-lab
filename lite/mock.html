Mock interview questions

Answer out loud against the clock, then score yourself on the rubric.

Project deep dive · 15 min

1. Walk me through the most technically complex project you have worked on. Then I will drill into one decision.

A strong answer covers:

  • One-minute pitch: problem, users, why it mattered, your role
  • Clear architecture with the hard part identified
  • Two or three decisions with alternatives and costs
  • Depth three levels down on at least one decision
  • Numbers from production
  • What went wrong and what you would change

Project deep dive · 10 min

2. Pick a system you built. What breaks first at 10× traffic, and how would you know before customers do?

A strong answer covers:

  • Names the real bottleneck with a reason
  • Uses numbers from the actual system
  • Detection: metric or alert that would fire
  • A staged fix, cheapest first
  • Honest about parts they did not own

Behavioral · 3 min

3. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a technical decision. What did you do?

A strong answer covers:

  • Specific situation and stakes
  • You understood the other side’s constraints
  • Used evidence/experiments, not volume
  • Outcome and relationship afterwards
  • What you would do differently

Behavioral · 3 min

4. Describe a production incident you owned from detection to follow-up.

A strong answer covers:

  • Detection path and time
  • Mitigated before diagnosing
  • Clear diagnosis with evidence
  • Systemic fix, not just a patch
  • Blameless learning and a shipped follow-up

Behavioral · 3 min

5. How have you raised the engineering bar on your team?

A strong answer covers:

  • Concrete mechanism (review norms, design docs, testing, mentoring)
  • Adoption, not mandate
  • Measured impact
  • Handled resistance

Behavioral · 3 min

6. Tell me about a project that failed or was cancelled.

A strong answer covers:

  • Honest account of your part
  • Early signals you missed or raised
  • Learning turned into a changed practice

Behavioral · 3 min

7. How do you decide when to build, buy, or adopt a library/service?

A strong answer covers:

  • Total cost of ownership
  • Core vs context
  • Lock-in and exit strategy
  • Security/compliance
  • A real example

Behavioral · 3 min

8. Tell me about a time you got buy-in for a change that people outside your team resisted.

A strong answer covers:

  • Understood the other side’s constraint first
  • Evidence: prototype, numbers or a pilot
  • Made it cheap to say yes
  • Outcome and the relationship afterwards
  • No villains

Behavioral · 3 min

9. Everything on your plate is urgent. Walk me through a time you had to prioritise, and what you said no to.

A strong answer covers:

  • An explicit rule (impact, risk, reversibility)
  • What was dropped or delayed, named
  • How stakeholders were told
  • Result and whether the call held up

Reliability · 10 min

10. A nightly settlement job must run exactly once, but you run three instances for availability. How do you make that safe?

A strong answer covers:

  • Lease-based leader election from an existing coordinator (etcd, Postgres, DynamoDB)
  • Renewal well within the TTL
  • Fencing token checked by the resource
  • Idempotent job steps so a rerun is safe
  • Alert when the job did not run

Reliability · 10 min

11. A Black Friday surge brings 3× normal traffic and your payment API starts timing out everywhere. What do you build so this does not happen again?

A strong answer covers:

  • Explains why latency collapses (queues, Little’s law, retries)
  • Bounded concurrency and queues
  • Priority load shedding: background work first
  • Retry budgets at one layer, with jitter
  • Load test and capacity plan with numbers

Reliability · 8 min

12. How would you roll out a risky change to the payment routing logic?

A strong answer covers:

  • Feature flag separating deploy from release
  • Canary with automated comparison against the baseline
  • Clear rollback trigger and owner
  • Reconciliation or shadow comparison for money correctness
  • Timing: avoid peak periods

Reliability · 8 min

13. Two services emit events about the same payment and a consumer merges them by timestamp. Refunds sometimes appear before payments. Explain and fix.

A strong answer covers:

  • Clock skew between machines
  • Why UTC does not fix it
  • Per-entity version or sequence from one authority
  • Consumer ignores stale versions
  • Mentions Lamport or vector clocks as the general tool

AI Era · 10 min

14. Your LLM feature broke overnight because the provider retired the model. Walk me through the incident and what you would change.

A strong answer covers:

  • Detection gap: health check did not call the model
  • Mitigation: switch to an evaluated model via config
  • Eval run before the permanent switch
  • Fallback model and real error surfaced in logs
  • Deprecation tracking with an owner

AI Era · 10 min

15. Product wants an assistant that can read a customer’s transactions and initiate payments for them. How do you design it safely?

A strong answer covers:

  • Least privilege, scoped to the user
  • Read and write separated; payments need explicit confirmation
  • Untrusted content treated as data (prompt injection)
  • Server-side validation of every tool call
  • Audit log, budgets and evals

Coding under pressure · 25 min

16. Live coding: given a list of account transfers, detect whether money can flow in a cycle back to the starting account. Narrate as you go.

A strong answer covers:

  • Clarifies input shape and edge cases
  • Models it as a directed graph
  • DFS with visiting/visited states (or Kahn’s algorithm)
  • States complexity
  • Tests a self-loop and a disconnected graph

Coding under pressure · 25 min

17. Live coding: return the k merchants with the highest total volume from a stream of payments. Then: what if it does not fit in memory?

A strong answer covers:

  • Counter plus a heap of size k, with complexity
  • Handles ties and k larger than the number of merchants
  • Follow-up: partition by merchant, or approximate (count-min sketch)
  • Tests

System Design · 35 min

18. Design a transaction history page for a bank app: 20M users, read-heavy, and users expect to see a payment right after making it.

A strong answer covers:

  • Numbers: rps, storage, read/write ratio
  • Read replicas plus read-your-writes for the user’s own payments
  • Pagination with stable cursors
  • Caching and its staleness rules
  • What happens when the core banking system is slow

System Design · 35 min

19. Design a webhook delivery platform for payment status changes.

A strong answer covers:

  • Requirements: at-least-once, retries, replay
  • Transactional outbox → queue → workers
  • HMAC signing + timestamp, secret rotation
  • Per-endpoint isolation, backoff, DLQ, circuit breaking
  • Ordering per entity + event ids for dedupe
  • Metrics and customer-facing dashboard

System Design · 35 min

20. Design a rate limiter used by many services.

A strong answer covers:

  • Algorithms: token bucket vs sliding window
  • Where: edge vs service vs client
  • Shared state (Redis + atomic ops), fail-open vs fail-closed
  • Per-tenant fairness and burst policy
  • Clear 429 + Retry-After
  • Observability

System Design · 35 min

21. Design a URL shortener that handles 10k writes/s and 100k reads/s.

A strong answer covers:

  • ID generation without collisions
  • Read-heavy: cache + CDN
  • Storage choice and partitioning
  • Abuse/expiry/analytics as async
  • Availability and failure modes

System Design · 35 min

22. Design a notification system (email/SMS/push) with user preferences.

A strong answer covers:

  • Channels as pluggable providers
  • Queue per channel/priority
  • Preferences, quiet hours, deduplication
  • Retries, provider failover, idempotency
  • Template/versioning and audit

Neonomics / Open Banking · 40 min

23. Design an account-data aggregator over hundreds of banks with very different APIs and rate limits.

A strong answer covers:

  • Adapter/anti-corruption layer per bank or standard
  • Canonical model + raw payload retention
  • Per-bank timeouts, breakers, bulkheads, token buckets
  • Refresh scheduler honouring PSD2 unattended limits
  • Consent/SCA state machine and reauth UX
  • Freshness flags, reconciliation, observability per bank

Neonomics / Open Banking · 35 min

24. How do you guarantee a payment is never initiated twice?

A strong answer covers:

  • Idempotency keys scoped per customer with unique constraint
  • Atomic claim + effect (or outbox)
  • Safe retries and timeouts with unknown outcome
  • Status polling/webhooks with monotonic state rules
  • Reconciliation with bank and duplicate detection alerts

Neonomics / Open Banking · 20 min

25. A user revokes consent. Walk through everything that must happen.

A strong answer covers:

  • Stop scheduled refreshes immediately
  • Revoke at bank/aggregator if possible
  • Delete/anonymise stored data per policy and law
  • Audit trail kept
  • Propagate to caches, customers (webhook) and analytics

Neonomics / Open Banking · 20 min

26. Every bank call starts failing at 03:00 with TLS errors. What do you check and what would you have built to prevent it?

A strong answer covers:

  • Shared-cause reasoning: certificates/CA chain
  • Immediate mitigation: rotate/renew, roll back
  • Expiry monitoring with long lead time
  • Runbook and secrets/HSM handling
  • Post-incident follow-ups

Neonomics / Open Banking · 15 min

27. How would you use AI in a regulated payments company, and where would you refuse to?

A strong answer covers:

  • Good fits: support triage, log/error explanation, categorisation, dev productivity
  • Hard rule: no model decisions on money movement without deterministic controls and human approval
  • Data protection: no PII to unapproved providers, redaction, DPAs
  • Evals, audit logs, fallbacks
  • Prompt injection risks with tools

Scale & Cloud · 30 min

28. Your Kafka consumer group is falling behind. Walk through diagnosis and fixes.

A strong answer covers:

  • Metric: lag and its trend, processing time per message
  • Bottleneck: consumer, downstream, hot partition, rebalances
  • Scale consumers up to the partition count
  • Batching, async I/O, idempotent writes
  • Backpressure, DLQ for poison messages
  • Plan partitions for future parallelism

Scale & Cloud · 30 min

29. Design a multi-AZ deployment on AWS with zero-downtime releases for a payments API.

A strong answer covers:

  • ALB, ≥2 AZs, health checks
  • ECS/EKS rolling or blue/green with auto rollback
  • Backward-compatible DB migrations
  • Aurora/RDS Multi-AZ, Secrets Manager, KMS
  • Autoscaling on leading metrics
  • Observability gating the rollout

Scale & Cloud · 20 min

30. How do you run Terraform safely in a team?

A strong answer covers:

  • Remote encrypted state with locking, state per env/service
  • PR plan review, saved plan applied by CI
  • Least-privilege CI roles, no console writes, drift detection
  • Modules with pinned versions
  • Protecting stateful resources (prevent_destroy), reading replacements carefully

Scale & Cloud · 25 min

31. Traffic grows 10× in six months. How do you find and remove bottlenecks?

A strong answer covers:

  • Load test and profile; find the real bottleneck
  • Stateless services + autoscaling
  • Cache, indexes, N+1 fixes, batching
  • Queues for async work; replicas then sharding
  • Rate limits, load shedding, capacity headroom
  • Cost awareness

Databases · 30 min

34. Model a payments system in Postgres that supports refunds and partial captures, and explain how you stop a double charge.

A strong answer covers:

  • Double-entry ledger or event history, append-only
  • Integer minor units + currency
  • Idempotency key with UNIQUE constraint
  • Constraints for captured <= authorised and refunded <= captured
  • Locking or atomic updates for concurrent captures
  • Reconciliation and audit

Databases · 20 min

35. Explain Postgres isolation levels and show how you would prevent two withdrawals from overdrawing an account.

A strong answer covers:

  • Read committed default, repeatable read = snapshot, serializable = SSI
  • Names lost update and write skew with an example
  • Atomic UPDATE with a balance guard, or SELECT FOR UPDATE
  • Retry loop for 40001 / 40P01
  • Keeps transactions short, no external calls inside

Databases · 25 min

36. A query that was fast last month is slow in production today. How do you diagnose and fix it?

A strong answer covers:

  • Reproduce, find it with pg_stat_statements / slow log
  • EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS): estimated vs actual rows, scan types
  • Stale statistics or data growth, ANALYZE
  • Index design: composite order, covering, partial
  • Locks / long transactions / bloat / connection saturation as alternatives
  • Safe rollout (CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY) and verification

Databases · 25 min

37. Describe a zero-downtime migration that splits a column on a 500-million-row table.

A strong answer covers:

  • Expand / contract phases
  • Constant default or nullable add, no table rewrite
  • Batched backfill with throttling
  • Dual write, then switch reads
  • NOT VALID then VALIDATE constraints, lock_timeout
  • Rollback plan at each step

Coding under pressure · 30 min

38. Live coding: merge overlapping intervals. Narrate your process from the first minute.

A strong answer covers:

  • Restated and clarified (touching intervals, empty input, ordering)
  • Gave an example and an edge case
  • Brute force first, then the sort-based approach
  • Clean code with small steps
  • Tested by hand and fixed bugs
  • Stated time and space complexity

Coding under pressure · 35 min

39. Live coding: given payment routes with fees between intermediaries, find the cheapest route. What do you do when you get stuck?

A strong answer covers:

  • Recognised weighted shortest path
  • Chose Dijkstra and justified non-negative weights
  • Used a heap, handled unreachable and src == dst
  • Narrated the plan before typing
  • Explained how to simplify when stuck
  • Complexity and follow-ups (negative weights, large graphs)

Full-stack · 25 min

40. Design authentication for a single-page app with a serverless backend and a Postgres database.

A strong answer covers:

  • Chose a flow (code + PKCE or passwordless code) and said why
  • Short-lived access tokens, refresh tokens, where each lives
  • Backend verifies tokens correctly (alg, signature, exp/aud/iss)
  • Authorisation enforced by the database (RLS) or the server, not the client
  • CSRF/XSS trade-offs of cookie vs header tokens
  • Logout, revocation and key rotation

Full-stack · 30 min

41. You proxy a rate-limited public API for thousands of users. How do you stay within quota and stay fast?

A strong answer covers:

  • Quantified the quota against traffic
  • Shared cache with TTLs per payload and versioned keys
  • Single-flight / fan-in and stale-while-revalidate
  • Poll only what is being watched
  • Failure handling: stale-if-error, token refresh, quota exhaustion
  • Metrics and alerts

Full-stack · 30 min

42. Design cross-device sync for an offline-first app. How do you handle conflicts?

A strong answer covers:

  • Local-first, network-independent saves
  • Server generation with compare-and-swap
  • Hash-based dedupe and three-way reconcile
  • Explicit conflict choice, no last-write-wins
  • Reset, stale client and account-switch edge cases
  • RLS boundary and size caps

Full-stack · 20 min

43. A user says they can see another user’s data after logging in. What do you check, in order?

A strong answer covers:

  • Reproduce and scope it (one user, one endpoint, one cache layer)
  • Shared caches: cache key, Vary, Cache-Control private/public on personalised responses
  • Missing tenant filter or RLS policy
  • Session handling (shared tokens, cookie scope)
  • Frontend caching namespaced by user
  • Fix, add a regression test, and plan disclosure
