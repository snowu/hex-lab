Scope, prototyping and playtesting

How small games actually get finished: prototypes that answer questions, vertical slices, and watching people play.

Prototype to answer a question

A prototype is not a smaller version of the game, it is an experiment: “Is dodging this attack fun?”, “Is grapple movement readable?” Build the cheapest thing that can answer it: grey boxes, placeholder sounds, one room. Throw it away if it fails; keep the learning.

Scope is the main killer

Most solo projects die by scope, not by skill. Habits that work:

  • Write a one-page design: pitch, loop, pillars, and a cut list ordered from “must ship” to “nice to have”. Cut from the bottom without guilt.
  • Build a vertical slice: one level at final quality with all main systems, to prove the loop and surface cost early.
  • Estimate honestly and multiply by 2 or 3. Content (levels, art, sound) is far more work than code.
  • Prefer systems that generate depth from few assets (combinable rules, procedural variation) over hand-made content.
  • Ship something tiny first. A finished 2-hour game teaches more than an unfinished epic.
  • Use jam constraints (48 hours, a theme, a limit like “one button”) to practise finishing.

Playtesting

  • Watch, do not explain. Sit behind the player and stay silent. Where they hesitate or repeat a mistake is where the design is unclear.
  • Ask about what they did and felt (“What were you trying to do there?”), not “did you like it?”.
  • Test early with ugly builds. Test with people who have never seen the game, five at a time, then fix the problems all five hit.
  • Record sessions (with permission) and log telemetry such as deaths per room and quit points.
  • Distinguish problem from solution: players are great at reporting problems and terrible at proposing fixes.

Template: a one-page cut list

  1. Must ship: core loop, 3 enemy types, 5 levels, save, pause, sound.
  2. Should ship: 2 extra weapons, boss, polished menu, settings (volume, shake, rebind).
  3. Nice to have: secrets, leaderboard, achievements, extra biome.
  4. Not now (write it down so you can stop thinking about it): online, level editor, mod support.

Review weekly. Anything in “must ship” that is not playable by the halfway point is a risk to tackle first.
