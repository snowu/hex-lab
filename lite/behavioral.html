Senior Playbook · lesson 2 of 4 · 10 min

Behavioral answers that land

STAR+ with impact and reflection.

Use STAR+: Situation (1 sentence), Task (what you owned), Action (the bulk, in “I” not “we”), Result (with a number), + Reflection (what you learned or changed).

Weak vs strong

Weak:   "We had performance problems, so we added caching and it got faster."
Strong: "Checkout p99 was 4.2s and we were losing ~3% of carts. I profiled it, found 60% of the
         time was repeated currency lookups, proposed a 5-minute cache with explicit invalidation,
         wrote the design doc, and got buy-in despite the team preferring a rewrite. p99 dropped to
         900ms. I'd now add a metric for cache hit rate from day one; we discovered a stale-rate bug late."

Keep each answer around 2 minutes. Practice with the mock interview page, and record yourself once. It is uncomfortable and very effective.

What senior behavioural rounds actually score

The questions sound generic; the rubric is not. Each one probes a specific senior competency, and a story that is fine for a mid-level role (“I implemented X”) scores low because the scope is too small.

CompetencyTypical questionWhat a strong answer contains
Influence without authority“How did you get buy-in for a change others resisted?”Whose constraint you understood first, the evidence you brought (a prototype, numbers, a pilot), how you made it cheap to say yes
Prioritisation“Everything is urgent. What do you do?”An explicit rule (customer impact, risk, reversibility), what you said no to and how you told people
Disagree and commit“Tell me about a decision you disagreed with.”You argued with data, the decision went the other way, you committed fully and set a check to revisit it
Mentoring / multiplying“How have you grown other engineers?”A named person or practice, what changed in their output, and that it outlasted you
Ambiguity“Tell me about a project with unclear requirements.”How you created clarity: the question you asked, the assumption you wrote down, the smallest slice you shipped to learn
Ownership“Tell me about something outside your remit.”You noticed it, nobody asked, you fixed the system and not only the symptom

The senior traps

  • “We” all the way through. Interviewers cannot hire your team. Say “we” for context, “I” for your decisions.
  • Too small. A bug you fixed alone is a mid-level story. Senior stories cross team boundaries or change how people work.
  • No tension. A story where everyone agreed and nothing went wrong shows nothing. Pick ones with a real trade-off or a real opponent.
  • Villains. Never make a colleague the bad guy. Describe their constraint fairly; that is the signal.
  • Rehearsed and rigid. Expect “what would you do differently?” and “what did the other person think?” after every story. Prepare the follow-ups, not a script.

“Why are you leaving?” and “Why us?”

Answer toward something, not away from something: what you want to work on next and why this team has it. One honest sentence about what is missing now is fine; criticism of your current employer is not. For “why us”, name something specific you read (their API docs, an engineering post, a product decision) and connect it to your experience.

Build the bank: the Story bank tags each story with these competencies and shows the gaps. Aim for at least one story per row of the table above, and two for the ones the role emphasises.

Answer key

1. Quick check back ↑

B. “The on-call team was against moving to the new queue because they would carry the pager. I started by asking what would make it safe for them.”

Influence without authority starts from the other side’s constraint. Authority, a manager’s backing, or “everyone agreed” all skip the skill being tested.

2. Quick check back ↑

C. “I understood their constraint, proposed an experiment, and we both changed our view based on the data. We kept the working agreement afterwards.”

Strong conflict stories show empathy, evidence, and a durable outcome, not winning.

3. Quick check back ↑

B. A real failure with real cost, your part in it, and a concrete system or habit you changed afterwards

Interviewers are looking for honesty and learning loops. A good failure story ends with a changed system, not a changed mood.

4. Check 1 back ↑

B. Reflection: what you learned or changed afterwards

The + is the learning loop: what you changed in a system or habit, not just how you felt.

5. Check 2 back ↑

B. Add a number: the before/after measurement and the business effect

Results need numbers (p99 4.2s → 900ms, ~3% of carts). “Much faster” is not a result.
