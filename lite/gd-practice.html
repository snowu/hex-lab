The practice ladder

A sequence of small, finishable projects that each add one skill, and how to practise deliberately.

The way to get good is to finish many small games. Each rung below is a weekend or less and adds one new idea. Do them in your engine of choice (Godot) and at least the first few from scratch in Zig or C++ with raylib or SDL so you feel the loop you normally never see.

  1. Pong: the game loop, input, AABB vs circle, scoring, a simple AI paddle. Add: ball speeds up per hit.
  2. Breakout: many entities, swept collision (no tunneling), levels from data.
  3. Flappy-style one-button game: gravity, a fixed step, procedural obstacles with a seeded RNG, restart in under a second.
  4. Snake: grid movement, input buffering, game states (menu, play, game over), save a high score (versioned file).
  5. Platformer room: tile collision per axis, coyote time, jump buffering, variable jump height, camera.
  6. Top-down shooter: spawn pools, spatial hash, steering enemies, juice (shake, hit stop, particles).
  7. Tower defence: pathfinding on a grid, waves from data, an economy with sinks and faucets.
  8. Roguelike room generator: cellular automata or BSP rooms, connectivity check, seeds shown on screen.
  9. A deckbuilder or puzzle game: state machines, undo (command pattern), data-driven content.
  10. Your own idea, scoped to one week, with a cut list and a vertical slice.

Deliberate practice

  • Set a timebox (a weekend, a jam) and ship, even if rough. Put it on itch.io.
  • After each project write three lines: what was slow, what you would change, what you learned.
  • Clone, then change one rule. Copying a known game removes design risk so you can focus on implementation, then the changed rule is your design exercise.
  • Read other people's source (open-source games, engine examples). Reading is half the craft.
  • Alternate: one project for code skills (from scratch), one for design/art skills (in the engine), one for finishing (a jam).
  • Pair every project with a pixel art task: draw its sprites yourself, even badly. See the pixel art track and the editor.

Checklist: “is it a finished game?”

  • Starts from a title screen and can be quit.
  • Has a win or lose condition and a restart that takes under 2 seconds.
  • Pause works. Volume and (if relevant) screen shake settings exist.
  • No crash for 30 minutes of random play (try mashing every button).
  • Exports and runs on a machine that does not have the editor installed.
  • One page: controls, credits, license for any assets.
